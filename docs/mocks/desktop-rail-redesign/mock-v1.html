Desktop rail — three arrangements

All three drawn at true 1366×768 and scaled to fit this page, so the proportions you see are the proportions you get. Today's build is shown first for comparison.

The constraint is vertical, not horizontal. At 1366×768 the board is height-limited: it fills the stage's full 677 px of height and stops, leaving 227 px of the SVG element as empty letterbox at the sides. Narrowing the rail therefore buys nothing at this size — only reclaiming height does. Every option below spends its effort on the vertical axis.

A 51 px topbar, 20 px of stage padding top and bottom, and a rail whose controls stop 339 px short of the bottom.

Where today's pixels actually go

  • 227 px of letterbox. .board-frame carries an aspect-ratio of the board's bounds but also max-width: min(100%, 900px), and the max-width wins — so the element is 946 px wide while the board inside paints only 719. Overlays anchored to the frame (the dice) end up against empty sea rather than the board's corner.
  • 91 px of chrome and padding. A 51 px topbar plus 20 px of stage padding top and bottom. Because the board is height-limited, each of those pixels comes straight off the board — and off the number tokens with it, since the board is an SVG with a viewBox.
  • 339 px of empty rail, all below the turn button.

Two fixes to fold into whichever option wins

  • player={current} → viewed on TradeBar and ActionBar. The rail currently renders against whoever's turn it is, so during a bot's 700 ms tick it re-renders against the bot's hand. That is the blinking — and the affordability states leak what the bot is holding.
  • The bank supply row is clipped — its last button ends at x=1426 on a 1366 px screen. It is reference data rather than a per-turn input, so it belongs behind the existing ? guide.

Open questions

  • Option C removes the full-width topbar. If four chips in a 2×2 grid reads as cramped, B is the safe stopping point at +5% linear.
  • The rail stays 380 px in all three. It could narrow to ~320 px, but at 1366×768 that adds nothing — the board is height-limited. It only helps on narrow, tall windows.
